### Q01 (A — End-to-end / cross-module synthesis)

> Trace the full lifecycle of a stock item from Sales Order through to final accounting: Sales Order, Delivery Note, and Sales Invoice. Given that a Delivery Note can be submitted well before the corresponding Sales Invoice, how does ERPNext ensure the cost of goods sold is booked exactly once rather than twice? Name the relevant functions, classes and files across all three doctypes.

**[0]** Sales Order itself posts no GL entries — it is not an accounting document; SalesOrder.on_submit() (sales_order.py) only handles credit limit, reserved qty, and approving-authority checks.

- ◐ **codegraph-jev** (partial): Candidate correctly states SO posts no GL/SLE and details on_submit calls, but never mentions credit limit or approving-authority checks named in the gold fact.
- ◐ **codegraph-laya** (partial): Candidate captures that SO posts no GL and highlights reserved qty, but omits credit limit and approving-authority checks mentioned in the gold fact.

**[1]** DeliveryNote.on_submit() (erpnext/stock/doctype/delivery_note/delivery_note.py) calls update_stock_ledger() (creating Stock Ledger Entries that reduce warehouse qty) and then make_gl_entries(), inherited from controllers/stock_controller.py, which books inventory (credit) against a provisional expense/COGS account for the delivered items.

- ✅ **codegraph-jev** (covered): Candidate describes DeliveryNote.on_submit() calling update_stock_ledger() (reducing warehouse qty) then make_gl_entries() via stock_controller which posts the COGS/inventory pair.
- ✅ **codegraph-laya** (covered): Candidate correctly identifies update_stock_ledger() creating SLEs and make_gl_entries() inherited from stock_controller.py booking inventory against the provisional SDBNB account.

**[2]** SalesInvoice.on_submit() (erpnext/accounts/doctype/sales_invoice/sales_invoice.py) calls update_billing_status_in_dn() and then make_gl_entries(), which goes through erpnext/accounts/doctype/sales_invoice/services/gl_composer.py (SalesInvoiceGLComposer).

- ✅ **codegraph-jev** (covered): Candidate states SalesInvoice.on_submit() calls update_billing_status_in_dn() and traces make_gl_entries() to SalesInvoiceGLComposer in the correct gl_composer.py file.
- ◐ **codegraph-laya** (partial): Candidate correctly traces make_gl_entries() through the gl_composer.py SalesInvoiceGLComposer, but never names update_billing_status_in_dn() as part of on_submit().

**[3]** The reconciliation step that prevents double-booking COGS is the composer's stock-delivered-but-not-billed handling: for each invoice line referencing a delivery_note/dn_detail, it looks up the DN's Stock Ledger Entry value and the DN item's expense_account, and if that account is the provisional/SDBNB account, it reverses the provisional DN-side booking and re-books it as real COGS tied to the invoice.

- ✅ **codegraph-jev** (covered): Candidate describes the SDBNB reclassification using the DN's SLE stock_value_difference and the DN item's expense_account, reversing the provisional booking and re-booking real COGS on the invoice.

**[4]** This means COGS is recognized exactly once even though the Delivery Note and Sales Invoice can be submitted at very different times — the invoice-side logic actively reverses what the delivery-note-side logic already posted, rather than both sides posting independently.

- ✅ **codegraph-jev** (covered): Candidate explicitly frames the SDBNB mechanism as a reclassification rather than double booking, matching the exactly-once claim despite different submission times.
- ✅ **codegraph-laya** (covered): Candidate explicitly states COGS is recognized exactly once via the invoice-side reversal of the DN-side provisional booking, matching the gold fact.

**[5]** If update_stock is set on the Sales Invoice itself (bypassing a separate Delivery Note), the invoice does its own update_stock_ledger() and stock-movement GL entries directly instead of going through the DN-reversal path.

- ✅ **codegraph-jev** (covered): Candidate states that when update_stock=1 (no DN link), the SI itself creates the SLE and books COGS directly, matching the gold fact.

### Q21 (E — Concurrency & database semantics)

- ✅ **codegraph-jev** (covered): Answer correctly identifies repost_gate's advisory_lock call with the exact key and the postgres-only condition with fallback.
- ✅ **codegraph-laya** (covered): Answer correctly names the function, session-level lock type, exact advisory_lock call with the same key tuple, and notes MariaDB gets nullcontext fallback.

**[1]** repost_gate is used as a context manager wrapping update_entries_after(...) inside repost_future_sle's loop, so row-level locks happen only after the advisory lock is acquired, avoiding lock-order deadlocks between concurrent reposts of the same item/warehouse.

- ✅ **codegraph-jev** (covered): Answer shows repost_gate wrapping update_entries_after inside repost_future_sle's loop and explains it avoids lock-order deadlocks.
- ✅ **codegraph-laya** (covered): Answer explicitly states the gate wraps update_entries_after inside repost_future_sle's loop and that it precedes row-level locks to avoid lock-order deadlocks.

**[2]** The synchronous repost_current_voucher submit path is NOT wrapped by repost_gate, to avoid blocking a document submit behind a background repost.

- ✅ **codegraph-jev** (covered): Answer states the synchronous submit path is not wrapped by repost_gate, matching the fact.
- ✅ **codegraph-laya** (covered): Answer directly states the submit path is excluded from the gate and gives the same rationale as the gold fact.

**[3]** BatchNoValuation.calculate_avg_rate in erpnext/stock/serial_batch_bundle.py takes a transaction-scoped advisory lock via frappe.db.transaction_advisory_lock keyed on ("batch-valuation", item_code, warehouse), only for outward transactions and only on Postgres.

- ✅ **codegraph-jev** (covered): Answer correctly identifies BatchNoValuation.calculate_avg_rate's transaction-scoped advisory lock keyed on batch-valuation, gated for outward transactions and postgres only.
